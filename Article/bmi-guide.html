Ideal BMI Guide

A complete reference on Body Mass Index (BMI) for men and women, adjusted by age, plus a practical food guide for maintaining a healthy weight.

1. What is BMI?

Body Mass Index (BMI) is a simple calculation using a person's height and weight to estimate whether their body weight is in a healthy range. It doesn't measure body fat directly, but it's a useful screening tool used worldwide by health organizations.

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ [height (m) × height (m)]

Example: A person who is 1.65 m tall and weighs 60 kg has a BMI of 60 ÷ (1.65×1.65) ≈ 22.0.

Note: These ranges are general guidelines from the World Health Organization (WHO) and apply mainly to adults. Athletes with high muscle mass may show a "high" BMI despite low body fat, so BMI should be read alongside other health indicators.

4. Ideal BMI by Gender

Women

The ideal BMI range for adult women is generally 18.5 – 24.9. Women naturally carry a slightly higher percentage of body fat than men (essential for hormonal and reproductive health), so body fat percentage guidelines differ even when the BMI range is the same.

Men

The ideal BMI range for adult men is also 18.5 – 24.9. Men typically carry more lean muscle mass, so at the same BMI, men often have lower body fat percentage than women.

12. Macronutrients: Carbohydrates, Fats & Protein

Carbohydrates

Function: main energy source; fiber also helps limit calorie intake, lower disease risk, and determines a food's glycemic index. Sources: sugar, flour, whole grains, brown/red rice, whole wheat pasta, legumes, vegetables, fruit. Deficiency: low energy, dizziness, nausea, weakness — extreme low-carb diets risk dehydration. Daily intake: about 45–65% of total daily calories (≈225–325 g/day on a 2,000-kcal diet).

Fats

Function: energy reserve, structural component, vitamin carrier, temperature regulation, supports brain, bone, liver, and lung health. Sources: fish, avocado, seeds, nuts, olive oil, eggs, yogurt, cheese, coconut. Daily intake: Men ~30 g/day · Women ~20 g/day.

13. Ideal Weight — Broca Formula (Alternative to BMI)

Unlike BMI, which is adjusted by age, the Broca formula calculates ideal weight differently for men and women.

Men: Ideal weight (kg) = [height (cm) − 100] − 10% × [height (cm) − 100]

Women: Ideal weight (kg) = [height (cm) − 100] − 15% × [height (cm) − 100]

Example (man, 170 cm): (170−100) − 10%×70 = 70−7 = 63 kg
Example (woman, 158 cm): (158−100) − 15%×58 = 58−8.7 = 49.3 kg

16. Realistic Step-by-Step Plan: From Obesity Back to a Healthy BMI

A safe, sustainable pace is about 0.5–0.75 kg (1–1.5 lb) per week, which usually means a daily calorie deficit of roughly 500–800 kcal. Losing even 5–10% of body weight already lowers blood pressure, blood sugar, and joint strain, so treat it as a series of small wins rather than one huge goal. Crash diets usually backfire: the weight comes back, often with lost muscle.

Your Personal Plan Calculator

The 10 Steps

  1. Get a starting point and a quick check-up Record your height, weight, BMI, and waist circumference. See a doctor first if your BMI is 30 or higher, you take regular medication, or you have conditions such as high blood pressure, diabetes, or joint pain. Ask for basic blood tests (blood sugar, cholesterol).
  2. Set staged goals instead of one big goal Stage 1: lose the first 5% of your body weight. Stage 2: reach 10%. Stage 3: continue until you reach a BMI under 25. Review your progress every 4 weeks.
  3. Create a moderate calorie deficit Aim for about 500 kcal/day below your maintenance level. Do not eat below roughly 1,200 kcal/day (women) or 1,500 kcal/day (men) without medical supervision. The easiest first cuts:
    • Replace sugary drinks (soda, sweet tea, bubble tea, juice with added sugar) with water or unsweetened tea
    • Swap deep-fried dishes for grilled, steamed, or boiled versions
    • Reduce rice/noodle portions by about one third and fill the space with vegetables
  4. Build a balanced plate Half the plate vegetables, a quarter lean protein, a quarter whole grains or other complex carbs, plus a small portion of healthy fat. Include protein at every meal (about 20–30 g) because it keeps you full and protects muscle. Pick foods from the food table in section 7.
  5. Eat on a rhythm Three regular meals plus one or two planned snacks (fruit, yogurt, a small handful of nuts). Eat slowly, stop when about 80% full, and avoid eating in front of a screen. Keep tempting snacks out of the house rather than relying on willpower.
  6. Move more, and build up gradually
    • Weeks 1–2: walk 10–15 minutes daily
    • Weeks 3–8: build up to 30 minutes of brisk walking, 5 days a week (150 minutes/week)
    • From week 4 on: add 2 strength sessions per week (chair squats, wall push-ups, resistance bands)
    • Week 9 onward: add cycling or swimming, and slowly work toward 200–300 minutes per week
    Low-impact options (walking, swimming, cycling) protect knees and ankles when carrying extra weight. Stop and see a doctor if you feel chest pain, severe breathlessness, or dizziness.
  7. Protect your sleep and manage stress Aim for 7–9 hours of sleep. Short sleep raises hunger hormones and cravings. Have a non-food plan for stressful moments (a short walk, calling a friend, stretching) and set a "kitchen closed" time in the evening.
  8. Track, then adjust Weigh yourself once a week at the same time of day, and measure your waist once a month. Log your meals for at least the first 8 weeks. Plateaus after 4–8 weeks are normal: recalculate your calorie needs at your new weight and add 10–15 minutes of daily activity instead of cutting food drastically.
  9. Plan for setbacks One bad meal or one bad weekend does not undo your progress. Get back on track at the very next meal instead of "starting over on Monday." Consistency over months matters far more than perfection on any one day.
  10. Reach your target, then maintain it Once your BMI is under 25, raise your calories slowly (about 100–200 kcal every 1–2 weeks) until your weight stays stable. Keep up your activity, keep weighing weekly, and act early if you regain 2–3 kg. Treat the first year of maintenance as part of the plan, since that is when most regain happens.
